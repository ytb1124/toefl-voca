[00:08.54]####_1_Woman:_#### We now turn to the phenomenon of the urban heat island effect. This occurs when urban areas experience
higher temperatures than their rural surroundings. The primary causes are, one: buildings, roads, and other human infrastructure that
absorb and retain heat; and two: reduced vegetation that would otherwise provide cooling through shade and transpiration—transpiration is
the release of water vapor into the air by plants.

[00:36.69] One contributing factor is the materials we use in urban construction. Materials like asphalt, concrete, and brick have high
thermal mass, meaning they store heat during the day and release it slowly at night. This results in higher nighttime temperatures in cities
compared to rural areas. Of course, air-conditioning units and cars also emit heat, and this further exacerbates the temperature difference.
[01:02.73] There are several consequences to the urban heat island effect. For one thing, it causes residents and businesses to rely more
heavily on air-conditioning, which increases energy consumption. Higher temperatures can also worsen air quality by increasing the
formation of ground-level ozone, which is a key component of smog. It also affects human health, vulnerable populations in particular.
[01:21.26] How do we mitigate the urban heat island effect? Increase green spaces in cities; use reflective materials in construction; and
implement so-called green roofs, which are just what they sound like—roofs covered with a soil base and vegetation.
