Sleep plays a critical role in memory formation and consolidation. During sleep, the brain processes and organizes information acquired
throughout the day. Studies have shown that sleep helps transform short-term memories into long-term memories. This process is essential
for learning and cognitive function.

Research conducted at several universities found that participants who had a good night’s sleep performed better on memory tasks than
those who were sleep deprived. For instance, individuals who slept after studying a list of words were more likely to recall the words the next
day. This suggests that sleep strengthens the neural connections in the brain.

There is also evidence that different stages of sleep contribute uniquely to memory consolidation. The rapid eye movement (REM) phase of
sleep is associated with emotional and procedural memory, while non—REM sleep, especially slow-wave sleep, is linked to declarative memory
—the conscious recall of facts and events. This distinction helps explain why certain types of memories are more affected by sleep than
others. However, the modern lifestyle often disrupts sleep patterns, leading to insufficient sleep. Factors such as stress, light exposure, and
irregular schedules can negatively impact sleep quality. As a result, memory formation and overall cognitive health can be compromised.
