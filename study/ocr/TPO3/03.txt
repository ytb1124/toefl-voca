The Science of Sleep

Sleep is a vital process that allows the body and mind to rest and rejuvenate. Scientists have long studied sleep to understand its functions
and benefits. Sleep is divided into several stages, including rapid eye movement (REM) and non-REM sleep. Each stage plays a crucial role in
maintaining overall health. REM sleep is associated with dreaming and memory consolidation, while non—REM sleep is important for physical

restoration and immune function.

One of the key functions of sleep is to support cognitive processes. During sleep, the brain processes and consolidates information acquired
throughout the day, which enhances learning and problem-solving abilities. Studies have shown that lack of sleep can impair cognitive
performance, leading to difficulties in concentration, decision-making, and memory.

Despite its importance, many people do not get enough sleep due to various factors such as stress, lifestyle choices, and medical conditions.
Chronic sleep deprivation is linked to a range of health issues, including obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and weakened immune
function. Therefore, prioritizing good sleep hygiene and addressing sleep disorders are essential for maintaining overall health and well-

being.
