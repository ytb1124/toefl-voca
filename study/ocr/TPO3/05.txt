Traffic flow is often analyzed through mathematical models, but human psychology plays a significant role in driving behavior. Drivers'
perceptions of time and stress levels can influence traffic conditions. Impatience in congested areas often leads to aggressive driving,
worsening traffic jams. Interestingly, phantom traffic jams occur without any apparent cause. These are triggered by small disruptions, such
as a single driver braking suddenly, leading to a ripple effect.

Studies by traffic psychologists show that varying stress levels among drivers contribute to inconsistent driving patterns. Stressful events, like
being late for work or facing a tight schedule, can cause drivers to make erratic decisions, impacting traffic flow. Drivers tend to react
differently to traffic signals depending on their psychological state. A calm driver is likely to maintain a steady speed, while a stressed driver
may frequently change lanes or speed up abruptly

Another aspect of traffic psychology involves route selection. Drivers often choose routes based on perceived traffic conditions rather than
actual data. This can lead to congestion on popular routes, even when alternative paths are more efficient. Traffic- management systems are
being developed to incorporate real-time psychological data to predict and alleviate traffic issues, aiming for smoother and safer road
experiences.
